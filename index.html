04 OCEAN Marine ecosystem

Ocean pollution is a system problem involving production, consumption, waste management, runoff, rivers, industry, fishing and human behavior.

The project separates pollution pressure from measured ocean leakage . There is no single scientifically defensible annual percentage series for “total ocean pollution” from 2000–2026.

4.Timeline:2000—2026

A timeline of
Ocean pollution.

2000–05

Plastic pollution enters the spotlight.

Plastic waste and marine litter become increasingly recognized as serious environmental problems. In 2002, Bangladesh became the first country to ban thin plastic bags after they were linked to blocked drainage and flooding.

2005–10

Ocean plastic becomes a global concern.

Research and public attention increasingly focus on plastic debris accumulating in oceans and coastal areas. Marine litter begins to be treated as a global environmental issue rather than only a local waste problem.

2010–15

Microplastics gain attention.

Scientists increasingly investigate microplastics and their effects on marine ecosystems. Monitoring and research into plastic pathways, marine debris and ocean health expand.

2015–20

Global action begins.

The UN Sustainable Development Goals establish "Goal 14: Life Below Water", including a target to significantly reduce marine pollution. In 2017, UNEP launches the Clean Seas campaign. Awareness grows rapidly through governments, communities and environmental campaigns.

2020–25

From awareness to coordinated action.

Marine plastic pollution becomes a major international policy issue. UNEP's research highlights the scale and impact of marine litter, while citizen-science projects and beach cleanups provide valuable pollution data. In 2022, countries agree to begin negotiating a egally binding international instrument to end plastic pollution.

2026

Data-driven ocean protection.

Ocean pollution awareness is increasingly connected with monitoring, data collection, mapping and community action. Digital tools can help identify polluted locations, organize evidence and connect pollution reports with cleanup efforts.

5.Basic Knowledge

Impressive,
without inventing data.

01

Ocean pollution includes plastics, sewage, nutrients, oil, chemicals, fishing gear and other pollutants measured differently.

02

Plastic production is an upstream pressure indicator, not a direct measurement of ocean leakage.

03

Great Pacific Garbage Patch density is localized and cannot represent the whole ocean.

04

Campaign metrics describe a specific campaign, not global awareness activity.
